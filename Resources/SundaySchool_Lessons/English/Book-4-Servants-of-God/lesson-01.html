A Family Flees from Famine

Download PDF Download Word

Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 4: Servants of God

Aim

To show the children that the refugee and famine problems we experience in Africa today are not new. People in Bible times experienced them, too.

Scripture Reading

Ruth 1:1-5

Memory Verse

Psalm 121:1

I lift up my eyes to the hills – where does my help come from?
NOTE: Over the next eight weeks, the children will learn Psalm 121 – one new verse each week. The theme of this Psalm is: The Lord is our Protector.

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

Everybody in the class probably knows families who have left their homes in the rural areas and walked many kilometres and with much difficulty to the towns. Ask them: Why do people do this? Answer: They come to find food and safety in the towns.

Main Points of the Lesson

1. During the times of the judges (Look, Listen & Live, Book 3) there was a famine in Israel. A man called Elimelech lived in Bethlehem with his wife, Naomi, and their two sons. The name Bethlehem means “House of Bread”. But there was no bread in Bethlehem! There was a famine. What would Elimelech do to feed his family?

2. Elimelech took his family to the country of Moab. That was about 80 kilometres south of Bethlehem. Show Picture 1. Explain that they had to walk there so they could take along only what they could carry.

3. In Moab there was food, but they were foreigners. The language, customs and religion were all different from their own. The Moabites did not worship the Lord God Almighty. Think of the difficulties Elimelech and his family would have experienced.

4. Sadness struck the family when Elimelech died. Fortunately for Naomi, she still had her two sons. They had married women from Moab in the meantime. But then both her sons died, too.

5. Ten years after she had come to Moab Naomi was left without any of her own people in that foreign country. She had no husband and no sons, just two foreign daughters-in-law. Her life had turned out very sad, she thought. She could see no hope for the future.

Our Response to the Story

Talk about the famines that have hit Africa in recent years, especially in Ethiopia and the Sudan. Perhaps you or one of the children has a personal experience to share.

In October 1988 there was no food in Juba, Southern Sudan. One month later the Lutheran World Federation began flying food into the town. Christians from all over the world gave food and money to make this airlift possible.

Instil the Memory Verse

This verse asks a question. What do you think is the answer? Give the children time to respond. We find the answer in the second verse of Psalm 121. Read verse 2 to them.

Israel’s neighbours were heathen tribes who worshipped idols instead of the one true God. They believed that these gods lived in the highest places, so they built their altars on the hilltops and mountains. But the writer of Psalm 121 knew that help would not come from the mountains; help comes from the living God alone.

Lesson - 1: A FAMILY FLEES FROM FAMINE

Scripture Reading: Ruth 1:1-5

1. Who Am I?

Choose from: Elimelech, Naomi, Moab

  1. I took my family away from the hungry land. Who am I? __________ 👉 Elimelech

  2. I was the wife who lost my husband and sons. Who am I? __________ 👉 Naomi

  3. I am the country where there was food. Who am I? __________ 👉 Moab

2. Yes or No?

Write Y for yes or N for no:

  1. Did Bethlehem have lots of bread at that time? ( ___ ) 👉 N

  2. Did the family walk a long way to Moab? ( ___ ) 👉 Y

  3. Did the Moabites worship the Lord? ( ___ ) 👉 N

3. Complete the Sentences

Word box: famine, help, hills

  1. There was no food because of a ____________. __________ 👉 famine

  2. The Psalm says, “I lift up my eyes to the ____________.” __________ 👉 hills

  3. Our true ____________ comes from God. __________ 👉 help

4. Discussion Questions

  1. Why did Elimelech leave Bethlehem?

    Sample answer: There was a famine and he wanted food for his family.

  2. Why do families still leave their homes today?

    Sample answer: To find food and a safe place to live.

  3. How can we help families who are hungry?

    Sample answer: We can share food, give, and pray for them.

5. Memory Verse:

Psalm 121:1 — “I lift up my eyes to the hills—where does my help come from?”

Psalm 46:1 — “God is our refuge and strength, an ever-present help in trouble.”

Craft 1: Bread Basket

Materials: Paper plate, brown paper, glue, crayons

Instructions:

  1. Fold the paper plate in half to make a basket.

  2. Cut loaves of bread from brown paper.

  3. Glue the loaves inside the basket.

  4. Write: “God is our help.”

Lesson Point: Bethlehem means “House of Bread”. God can give us food when we need it.

Craft 2: Walking Family Strip

Materials: Long paper strip, crayons, scissors

Instructions:

  1. Fold the strip like a fan.

  2. Draw one person on the front fold, with hands and feet touching the edges.

  3. Cut around the shape and open it up.

  4. Colour the family walking together.

Lesson Point: Elimelech’s family walked together to find food.

Game 1: Share the Bread

How to Play:

  1. Put paper bread pieces in a pile.

  2. One child at a time gives a piece to a friend.

  3. Keep going until everyone has some.

Lesson Point: We can share what we have with those who are hungry.

Game 2: Pack Your Bag

How to Play:

  1. Give each child a small bag.

  2. Call out “Pack!” and let them choose from a pile of items what they can carry.

  3. Talk about why they could not take everything.

Lesson Point: Refugees can only take what they can carry, so they need our help.
