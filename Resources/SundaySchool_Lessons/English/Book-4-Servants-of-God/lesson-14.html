David and the Giant Goliath

Download PDF Download Word

Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 4: Servants of God

Aim

To show the children how God helped his servant David defeat a giant when it looked impossible.

Scripture Reading

1 Samuel 17:1-54

Memory Verse

Psalm 1:3

He is like a tree planted by streams of water …

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

As you know different people grow to different heights. Some people grow very tall; others remain short. Show me the height of the tallest person you have ever seen. Show the class a maize stalk or a stick of about three meters long. Have you ever seen anyone this tall?

Main Points of the Lesson

1. Goliath was as tall as this stick and, of course, much wider. He was a giant. He belonged to the tribe of the Philistines, who were at war with Israel. Every day he challenged the Israelites to come and fight him. But all the soldiers were terrified of him (17:1-11).

2. Describe how David’s father sent him to visit his elder brothers in the army camp and take them some food. David heard Goliath’s challenge to Israel and their God, and he saw how frightened King Saul’s men were. He wondered why they were so afraid of this man who did not know the living God. He knew that even a huge giant is powerless against the power of God (17:17-25).

3. David’s eldest brother tried to make him quiet. In the end King Saul heard that this young man was willing to fight the giant, and he sent for David. Saul did not think David had a chance against Goliath, who was an experienced warrior. But David told the king that he put his trust in God. Show Picture 13 again. As God had helped him to protect his sheep from wild animals, so God would help him to protect his people from that giant (17:26-37).

4. Saul offered David his own armour to protect him, but it was much too big and heavy. Instead, he simply went to the river and selected five stones for his sling (17:38-40).

5. Show Picture 14. See the differences between Goliath and David – in size, in armour and in weapons. Most importantly, Goliath was fighting against the living God, while David was his servant (17:45).

6. Goliath was very angry to see that Israel was sending a mere youth to fight him, and without any armour or serious weapon, too! David told Goliath he was the servant of the living victorious God (17:41-47).

7. Tell the class how David triumphed over Goliath with a stone from his sling. When the Philistines saw that the giant was dead they fled. The Israelites won the battle and David kept Goliath’s sword (17:48-54).

Our Response to the Story

We are often frightened of things that look very big or powerful. Goliath did not frighten David, even though he was large and appeared to be so powerful. David acted in the power of God to overcome Goliath. God has given us, his servants, the same power to overcome the attacks and temptations of evil (1 Cor 10:13).

Instil the Memory Verse

Review the verses of Psalm 1 the children have learnt so far.

Lesson - 14: DAVID AND THE GIANT GOLIATH

Scripture Reading: 1 Samuel 17:1-54

1. Match the Following

A B
Goliath A huge Philistine giant
David Trusted the living God
Five stones Picked up for the sling
Saul’s armour Too big and heavy

Answers:
Goliath → A huge Philistine giant
David → Trusted the living God
Five stones → Picked up for the sling
Saul’s armour → Too big and heavy

2. Brave or Afraid? B/A

Write B for brave trust or A for afraid:

  1. Trusting God when something looks too big. ( ___ ) 👉 B

  2. Hiding because we are scared. ( ___ ) 👉 A

  3. Saying “God will help me.” ( ___ ) 👉 B

3. Complete the Sentences

Word box: God, stones, giant

  1. Goliath was a huge ____________. __________ 👉 giant

  2. David picked up five smooth ____________. __________ 👉 stones

  3. David won because ____________ helped him. __________ 👉 God

4. Discussion Questions

  1. Why was David not afraid of Goliath?

    Sample answer: He trusted God to help him.

  2. What big things frighten us?

    Sample answer: Answers will vary.

  3. What can we do when we are afraid?

    Sample answer: Pray and trust God.

5. Memory Verse:

Psalm 1:3 — “He is like a tree planted by streams of water, which yields its fruit in season and whose leaf does not wither. Whatever he does prospers.”

Psalm 56:3 — “When I am afraid, I put my trust in you.”

Craft 1: Five Smooth Stones

Materials: Paper, crayons, glue, card

Instructions:

  1. Cut five round shapes from paper.

  2. Colour them grey.

  3. Write one thing God helps with on each.

  4. Glue them on a card.

Lesson Point: God helps us with big and small problems.

Craft 2: David’s Shepherd Bag

Materials: Paper bag, crayons, string

Instructions:

  1. Decorate a small paper bag.

  2. Tie a string handle on it.

  3. Draw five stones on the front.

  4. Write: “God is bigger.”

Lesson Point: David trusted God more than armour or weapons.

Game 1: Giant Steps

How to Play:

  1. The leader takes “giant” steps.

  2. Children take “small” steps toward the leader.

  3. They say “God is greater!” as they arrive.

Lesson Point: God’s power is greater than any giant.

Game 2: Tower Tumble

How to Play:

  1. Stack paper cups as a tall “giant” tower.

  2. Children take turns tossing a soft paper ball at the cups.

  3. Say “God helps me” each time.

Lesson Point: David depended on God, not on his own strength.
