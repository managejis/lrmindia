Saul Tries to Kill David

Download PDF Download Word

Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 4: Servants of God

Aim

To show the children how God protected his servant David when King Saul became jealous of him.

Scripture Reading

1 Samuel 18:1-16; 20:1-42; 22:1-2

Memory Verse

Psalm 1:3

(continue learning v 3) He is like a tree planted by streams of water, which yields its fruit in season and whose leaf does not wither.

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

Ruta and Peris were two little sisters. Ruta received a beautiful new dress for her birthday. Peris was so upset by that that she cried. She wanted a new dress, too, but it was not her birthday. She tried to tear her sister’s new dress. Peris was very jealous of Ruta, as she had something Peris wanted, but could not have.

Main Points of the Lesson

1. King Saul became very jealous of David. That sounds surprising – a king becoming jealous of a shepherd boy! But David had killed the giant Goliath and had a high position in the army. The women of Israel began to make up songs praising David above the king. This made Saul very angry (18:5-9).

2. David also became great friends with Saul’s eldest son, Jonathan (18:1-5).

3. King Saul was so jealous of David that he began to lose control of himself. One day, as David was playing music to calm the king’s nerves, Saul threw his spear at David. David dodged it and the spear hit the wall. Saul tried again, but again David escaped (18:10-11). Show Picture 15.

4. After that day Saul tried to kill David many times but many times his son Jonathan helped David escape. At one special feast David was supposed to eat with the king and his family. David did not know if it was safe to come or not. He did not understand why Saul was so angry at him. Jonathan offered to help David and they worked out a secret plan. Jonathan would warn him if there was danger (20:1-23).

5. When Jonathan went out to the field with his servant to practise with his bow and arrows, David hid behind a pile of rocks. When David heard Jonathan tell his servant to go look for the furthermost arrows, he knew that was Jonathan’s signal to tell him it was not safe to stay near Saul (20:35-40).

6. After saying goodbye to Jonathan, David spent the next few years in the desert to escape from Saul (22:1-2).

Our Response to the Story

1 Samuel 18:12 shows the difference between David and Saul. The Lord was with David. He put his trust in God and lived in obedience to the Lord in spite of many problems. Saul, on the other hand, was proud, jealous and disobedient. As a result he lived in fear and anger. Saul was the king and David only a poor, hunted man, but David was the one who had the privilege of God’s company.

Jealousy may begin in a small way but it almost always grows and grows. In the end it can rule a person’s life and lead to great evil. Which direction is your life going? Are you a Saul or a David?

Instil the Memory Verse

Review the verses of Psalm 1 the children have learnt so far.

Lesson - 15: SAUL TRIES TO KILL DAVID

Scripture Reading: 1 Samuel 18:1-16; 20:1-42; 22:1-2

1. Jealous or Thankful? J/T

Write J for jealous or T for thankful:

  1. Being happy when a friend wins. ( ___ ) 👉 T

  2. Being angry because someone has more. ( ___ ) 👉 J

  3. Thanking God for what I have. ( ___ ) 👉 T

2. Choose the Right Answer

Circle a), b) or c):

  1. Who was David’s best friend?
    a) Jonathan b) Goliath c) Jesse 👉 a) Jonathan

  2. Why was Saul jealous?
    a) David was praised b) David had a palace c) David was tall 👉 a) David was praised

3. Missing Letters

Fill in the missing letters:

  1. Saul was j_ _ lo _ s of David. __________ 👉 jealous

  2. Jonathan helped David to es_ _ _ e. __________ 👉 escape

  3. The Lord was w_ _ h David. __________ 👉 with

4. Discussion Questions

  1. What happened because Saul was jealous?

    Sample answer: He became angry and unkind.

  2. How did Jonathan show he was a good friend?

    Sample answer: He helped David stay safe.

  3. What can we do when we feel jealous?

    Sample answer: Thank God and be happy for others.

5. Memory Verse:

Psalm 1:3 — “He is like a tree planted by streams of water, which yields its fruit in season and whose leaf does not wither.”

Proverbs 17:17 — “A friend loves at all times.”

Craft 1: Friendship Heart

Materials: Red paper, scissors, crayons, glue

Instructions:

  1. Cut two hearts.

  2. Write “Jonathan” and “David”.

  3. Glue the hearts side by side on card.

  4. Write: “A friend loves at all times.”

Lesson Point: Jonathan was a loyal friend to David.

Craft 2: Arrow Message

Materials: Card, crayons

Instructions:

  1. Cut an arrow from card.

  2. Colour the arrow.

  3. Write a kind message on it.

  4. Give it to a friend.

Lesson Point: Jonathan’s arrows helped David. We can use words to encourage friends.

Game 1: Happy for You

How to Play:

  1. A child wins a small paper prize.

  2. The rest cheer and say “Well done!”

  3. Swap so each child has a turn.

Lesson Point: We should be glad when others are blessed.

Game 2: Secret Signal

How to Play:

  1. Two children make a secret friendly sign.

  2. Others guess what it means.

  3. Talk about how Jonathan helped David.

Lesson Point: Friends help each other stay safe.
