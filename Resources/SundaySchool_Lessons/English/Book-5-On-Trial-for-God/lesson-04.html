Elisha and the Blind Army

Download PDF Download Word

Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 5: On Trial for God

Aim

To show the children that Elisha not only trusted in God to overcome his enemies; he also was not interested in revenge. As God forgives us, we should forgive those who sin against us and leave judgment to God.

Scripture Reading

2 Kings 6:18-23

Memory Verse

Exodus 20:4

(the Ten Commandments) “You shall not make for yourself an idol in the form of anything in heaven above or on the earth beneath or in the waters below.”
NOTE: Read verses 8-17, too, to review the background of today’s reading.

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

What do you think does a person need in order to be a good soldier? Possible answers: a strong body, good training, good eyesight, obedience to commanding officers.

Main Points of the Lesson

1. Elisha was facing the king of Syria’s powerful army – one man against a strong fighting force that had come to take him prisoner. What could he do? Elisha did the best and most powerful thing he could. He prayed to God. He asked God to make the Syrian soldiers blind, and God did (2 Ki 6:18).

2. Show Picture 4. Blind soldiers are useless, no matter how strong or well trained they are. Describe how Elisha tricked these Syrian soldiers, who now couldn’t see where they were going, and led them straight to Samaria, the capital city of Israel (6:19).

3. Elisha prayed again and God restored the Syrian soldiers’ sight (6:20). How ashamed and frightened these soldiers must have been!

4. The king of Israel immediately wanted to kill them, but he listened to Elisha’s wise words. God had worked miracles in that war through Elisha, and Israel had been spared. Now they should in their turn spare those Syrian soldiers and take no revenge (6:21-22).

5. To their great surprise the Syrian soldiers were given a feast and then sent home. As a result the Syrians stopped attacking Israel (6:23). Killing those soldiers, on the other hand, would have prolonged the war.

Our Response to the Story

Elisha’s God is our God, too. He is greater than any enemy we may encounter.

Those who trust in the living God should not seek revenge. The New Testament has some very practical advice about how we should treat our enemies. Read Matthew 5:44 aloud.

When I read you the next few verses, keep in mind that the Roman government was persecuting the Christians when Paul wrote these words to the church in Rome: Read Romans 12:17-21 out loud.

How do you treat people who treat you badly or are against you? Do you look for an opportunity to take some kind of revenge or perhaps say bad things about them? This is the way of the world, not the way Jesus Christ wants his followers to live.

Instil the Memory Verse

Review all the verses of Exodus 20 learnt so far.

Lesson - 4: ELISHA AND THE BLIND ARMY

Scripture Reading: 2 Kings 6:18-23

1. True or False?

Write T for True or F for False:

  1. Elisha prayed to God for help. ( ___ ) 👉 T

  2. Elisha wanted to punish the Syrian soldiers. ( ___ ) 👉 F

  3. The king of Israel gave the soldiers a feast. ( ___ ) 👉 T

2. Put the Story in Order

a) They ate a feast and went home.
b) The Syrians came to take Elisha.
c) Elisha led them to Samaria.
d) God made the soldiers unable to see.

👉 Correct Order:

  1. The Syrians came to take Elisha.

  2. God made the soldiers unable to see.

  3. Elisha led them to Samaria.

  4. They ate a feast and went home.

3. Unscramble the Words

Write the correct word:

  1. yarpde (what Elisha did) ____________ __________ 👉 prayed

  2. sfate (what the soldiers were given) ____________ __________ 👉 feast

  3. evgirof (what we should do to enemies) ____________ __________ 👉 forgive

4. Discussion Questions

  1. Why did Elisha not take revenge?

    Sample answer: He trusted God and showed kindness and mercy to enemies.

  2. What happened after the Syrians were fed and sent home?

    Sample answer: They stopped attacking Israel.

  3. What does Jesus tell us to do to people who are unkind to us?

    Sample answer: To love them and pray for them.

5. Memory Verse:

Exodus 20:4 — “You shall not make for yourself an idol in the form of anything in heaven above or on the earth beneath or in the waters below.”

Ephesians 4:32 — “Be kind and compassionate to one another.”

Craft 1: Feast Plate

Materials: Paper plate, coloured paper, glue, crayons

Instructions:

  1. Colour the plate.

  2. Cut paper shapes of bread, fruit and fish.

  3. Glue them on the plate.

  4. Write: “Be kind to everyone.”

Lesson Point: God wants us to show kindness, even to enemies.

Craft 2: Peace Dove

Materials: White paper, scissors, crayons, string

Instructions:

  1. Fold paper and cut out two wings.

  2. Draw a dove’s body and glue the wings on.

  3. Write: “Forgive, as God forgives.”

  4. Hang it with string.

Lesson Point: We forgive because God forgives us.

Game 1: Kindness Chain

How to Play:

  1. Children sit in a circle.

  2. Each says one kind thing to do for someone who was unkind.

  3. Link paper strips into a chain as they speak.

Lesson Point: Kindness is stronger than revenge.

Game 2: Guess the Kindness

How to Play:

  1. A child acts out a kind deed without speaking.

  2. The others guess what it is.

  3. Take turns.

Lesson Point: We can choose kind actions every day.
