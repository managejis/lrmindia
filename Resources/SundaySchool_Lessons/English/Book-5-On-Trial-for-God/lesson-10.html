Esther and the King

Download PDF Download Word

Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 5: On Trial for God

Aim

To show the children how God prepared Esther for the work he had planned for her.

Scripture Reading

Esther 1:1-12; 2:1-18

Memory Verse

The Ten Commandments

Review Exodus 20:1-7.

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

What do you think makes a person beautiful? There may be some giggles over this, but Sunday school is a good place to learn that God has created some people with very good looks. Beauty is given to glorify God and praise him. God made the world and the creatures who inhabit it (Ps 8:1). Our stories for the next three weeks are about a young woman who became a queen because of her beauty.

Main Points of the Lesson

1. King Xerxes of Persia was very powerful. He ruled over lands from India to the Sudan. He was very rich. He gave a feast for all his governors and officials, showing off all his wealth. It lasted six months! (Est 1:1-4).

2. Later the king gave a feast for everyone in his capital city of Susa. That feast lasted a week. At the feast his Queen refused to appear before the guests, probably because they were very drunk. King Xerxes was so angry that he dismissed her as queen (1:5-12, 16-19).

3. A great search was held to find the most beautiful girl to become the new queen (2:14).

4. Esther was a young Jewish girl from the country of Israel. Her parents had died, so her uncle Mordecai looked after her. Esther and Mordecai had been captured and taken to Persia with many other Jews when Persia’s army conquered Israel. Esther was absolutely beautiful (2:5-8).

5. Esther was chosen to go to the palace to be given a year’s beauty treatment. She not only had good looks, but also a good heart; therefore, she got on well with those in charge (2:9, 12).

6. When it was Esther’s turn to be seen by the king he liked her more than anyone else and chose her to be his new queen (2:15, 17-18). Show Picture 10.

7. All this time Mordecai kept giving Esther advice, and she listened to him. Mordecai told Esther not to tell anyone she was a Jew who had been brought as a prisoner from Israel (2:10-11).

Our Response to the Story

King Xerxes was a very powerful and wealthy man. He could be very ruthless, too. He had dismissed his queen because she would not do as he ordered. Esther was a very young girl whose parents had died and whose people had been taken prisoner in war. She had very little power except that which her beauty and character gave her. But as a Jew she knew how God had cared for and protected her people in the past. So she left home and went to live a completely different life in the royal palace. That must have taken much courage. Esther is an example of trust and obedience.

Lesson - 10: ESTHER AND THE KING

Scripture Reading: Esther 1:1-12; 2:1-18

1. True or False?

Write T for True or F for False:

  1. Esther was a Jewish girl. ( ___ ) 👉 T

  2. Her uncle Mordecai looked after her. ( ___ ) 👉 T

  3. Esther was chosen for her beauty only, not her good heart. ( ___ ) 👉 F

2. Who Am I?

Choose from: Xerxes, Mordecai, Esther

  1. I was the powerful king of Persia. Who am I? __________ 👉 Xerxes

  2. I became queen and I listened to my uncle. Who am I? __________ 👉 Esther

  3. I told Esther not to say she was a Jew. Who am I? __________ 👉 Mordecai

3. Choose the Right Answer

Circle a), b) or c):

  1. Where did Esther go to live?
    a) A palace b) A boat c) A tent 👉 a) A palace

  2. How long was Esther’s beauty treatment?
    a) A week b) A month c) A year 👉 c) A year

4. Discussion Questions

  1. Why did Esther need courage to go to the palace?

    Sample answer: She left her home and lived a very different life.

  2. What made Esther special besides her looks?

    Sample answer: She had a good heart and listened to advice.

  3. What might God have planned for Esther?

    Sample answer: To help and save her people.

5. Memory Verse:

Exodus 20:2-3 — “I am the Lord your God, who brought you out of Egypt, out of the land of slavery. You shall have no other gods before me.”

Psalm 138:8 — “The Lord will fulfil his purpose for me.”

Craft 1: Esther’s Crown

Materials: Gold paper, scissors, glue, crayons

Instructions:

  1. Cut a long gold strip.

  2. Cut zigzag points along the top.

  3. Decorate with coloured stars.

  4. Wrap and glue it to fit your head.

Lesson Point: God prepared Esther for his special plan.

Craft 2: Palace Picture

Materials: Paper, crayons, glue, scraps

Instructions:

  1. Draw a big palace with towers.

  2. Add gold paper for doors.

  3. Draw Esther in a window.

  4. Write: “God has a plan for me.”

Lesson Point: God has a plan for each of us.

Game 1: Queen’s Walk

How to Play:

  1. One child wears a paper crown and walks slowly.

  2. The others bow gently.

  3. Ask: “Who is the real King? God!”

Lesson Point: We bow only to God.

Game 2: Good Heart Game

How to Play:

  1. Read kind and unkind actions aloud.

  2. Children stand for kind and sit for unkind.

  3. Talk about a good heart.

Lesson Point: God looks for a good heart.
