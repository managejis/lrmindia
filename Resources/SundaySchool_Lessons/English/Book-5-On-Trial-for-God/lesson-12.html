Esther’s Feast

Download PDF Download Word

Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 5: On Trial for God

Aim

To show the children how God answered his people’s prayers.

Scripture Reading

Esther 5:1-14; 7:1-10; 9:1-4

Memory Verse

Exodus 20:9-10

(the Ten Commandments) “Six days you shall labour and do all your work, but the seventh day is a Sabbath to the Lord your God. On it you shall not do any work …”

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

What does it feel like when you are really frightened? Suggested answers: Your mouth goes dry, you feel as if there is a big knot in your stomach, your knees might even be knocking together.

Main Points of the Lesson

1. Queen Esther had a very dangerous task to do, but she had all the Jewish people praying for her. She had to go and see the king even though he had not called for her. As we saw last time, this could mean death for her unless he held out his golden sceptre to her (Est 4:16).

2. Describe how Esther dressed herself beautifully and went to see King Xerxes. He did hold out his sceptre to her; in fact, he was very pleased to see her (5:1-3)! Show Picture 10.

3. Esther wisely did not tell the king of her great problem. She just invited him and his prime minister Haman to a feast that she had prepared for them. The king agreed immediately (5:4-5). After they had enjoyed the food and drink Esther asked them to come again the next night (5:6-8).

4. Haman was very proud to have been invited to a feast with the king and queen. He boasted about it. But his happiness turned to anger when he remembered Mordecai, who still refused to bow to him even though he was so important. So Haman and his family erected a special gallows as high as the city walls, where they would hang Mordecai when the day came to kill all the Jews (5:9-14).

5. At the second feast the king asked again, “Queen Esther, what do you want? Ask me anything, even half my kingdom, and it shall be done.”

The following verses, Esther 7:1-6, are very dramatic. Learn the words of the conversation by heart and act the parts of King Xerxes and Queen Esther. Remind the class that Esther was a Jew. She was pleading for her people’s lives as well as her own. Show Picture 12.

6. The king was very angry when he heard of Haman’s wicked plans. He realised that Haman had tricked him. Haman was hanged on the gallows he had built for Mordecai, and the Jewish people were saved (7:7, 9-10; 9:1-4). God was with Esther in her difficult task and he answered his people’s prayers. Today the Jews still have a special feast day to remember Queen Esther’s act of obedience and courage (9:26-28).

Our Response to the Story

“God stepping in to save South Africa” – this was the heading in a newspaper after 25 000 people from the province of Natal in South Africa met in a sports stadium for a Jesus Peace Rally to pray for the government elections. While these thousands prayed a meeting between opposing tribal and political leaders took place. Their prayers were answered, and the elections were peaceful, with all groups being represented. God protected the people of South Africa from civil war.

God still hears and answers prayers today.

Instil the Memory Verse

Recite all the commandments learnt so far.

Lesson - 12: ESTHER’S FEAST

Scripture Reading: Esther 5:1-14; 7:1-10; 9:1-4

1. Put the Story in Order

a) The king learned the truth and the Jews were saved.
b) The Jews fasted and prayed for Esther.
c) Esther invited the king and Haman to a feast.
d) Esther went to see the king.

👉 Correct Order:

  1. The Jews fasted and prayed for Esther.

  2. Esther went to see the king.

  3. Esther invited the king and Haman to a feast.

  4. The king learned the truth and the Jews were saved.

2. Yes or No?

Write Y for Yes or N for No:

  1. Did the king hold out his golden sceptre to Esther? ( ___ ) 👉 Y

  2. Did God answer the people’s prayers? ( ___ ) 👉 Y

  3. Did Esther tell the king her problem at the first feast? ( ___ ) 👉 N

3. Choose the Right Answer

Circle a), b) or c):

  1. What did Esther prepare for the king?
    a) A feast b) A song c) A boat 👉 a) A feast

  2. Who saved the Jewish people?
    a) Haman b) God c) The guards 👉 b) God

4. Discussion Questions

  1. Why was Esther’s task dangerous?

    Sample answer: She went to the king without being called.

  2. How did God answer the people’s prayers?

    Sample answer: He kept the Jews safe and showed the king the truth.

  3. What can we do when we feel frightened?

    Sample answer: Pray and trust God.

5. Memory Verse:

Exodus 20:9-10 — “Six days you shall labour and do all your work, but the seventh day is a Sabbath to the Lord your God.”

Psalm 120:1 — “I call on the Lord in my distress, and he answers me.”

Craft 1: Feast Table Picture

Materials: Paper, crayons, coloured paper, glue

Instructions:

  1. Draw a long table.

  2. Cut out plates and fruit.

  3. Glue them on the table.

  4. Write: “God answers prayer.”

Lesson Point: God hears and answers his people’s prayers.

Craft 2: Prayer Heart

Materials: Red paper, scissors, crayons, string

Instructions:

  1. Cut a large heart.

  2. Write one thing you will pray for.

  3. Punch a hole and tie a string.

Lesson Point: We can pray for others in danger.

Game 1: Knees Knocking

How to Play:

  1. Ask: “How do we feel when we are scared?”

  2. Children act scared, then take a deep breath and say “God is with me.”

  3. Repeat.

Lesson Point: God gives courage when we are afraid.

Game 2: Answered Prayers

How to Play:

  1. Children name prayers God has answered.

  2. For each one, clap three times.

  3. Thank God together.

Lesson Point: God still answers prayers today.
