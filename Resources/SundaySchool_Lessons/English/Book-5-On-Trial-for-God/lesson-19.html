Nehemiah Before the Great King

Download PDF Download Word

Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 5: On Trial for God

Aim

To show the children how God answered Nehemiah’s prayer.

Scripture Reading

Nehemiah 1:1-11; 2:1-9

Memory Verse

Exodus 20:13

(the Ten Commandments) “You shall not murder.”

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

Have you ever tried to keep news to yourself? If it’s good news you have to be careful not to show happiness on your face or in the way you behave. Bad news is far more difficult to keep to yourself.

Main Points of the Lesson

1. Nehemiah had some very bad news. He was a Jew who trusted in God. He was a slave in Babylon, some time after Daniel. News was brought to Nehemiah that Jerusalem, the capital city of his ancestors, was lying in ruins, and people who lived there were experiencing great trouble (Neh 1:1-3).

2. Nehemiah took his sadness to God and prayed. He worshipped God and reminded God of his promises to Israel. He confessed the sins of his people, who had turned their backs on God. Finally he asked God to help him get assistance from the powerful King Artaxerxes. Nehemiah prayed like this for several months (1:4-11).

3. Nehemiah was a trusted servant of that foreign king. His job was to taste the king’s wine before he drank it, in case it was poisoned. No-one was allowed to look sad in the king’s presence. Show Picture 19 and describe how Nehemiah approached the king (2:1-4a).

4. Nehemiah silently prayed to God before he answered King Artaxerxes’s question. He was ready with his request for permission to go to Jerusalem and for building supplies. During the time he had spent in prayer beforehand, Nehemiah had made good plans. God then answered Nehemiah’s prayers, and Artaxerxes granted his requests. He also sent soldiers along to protect Nehemiah on his journey (2:4b-9).

Our Response to the Story

Some of you are probably six years old. This is how long a group of American Christians have been praying for the Sandawe people in America to hear God’s Word. God answered their prayers – and a small church has been planted there. One of the first to believe was a middle-aged man. He called at the missionary’s house and pulled out two little Christian tracts that he had been reading for a month. He said, “I have stayed with these little books for two months and I’ve read them and read them. I’ve talked with my wife about them. Now I’ve come here to be saved.” (Source: AIM International.)

God heard and answered Nehemiah’s prayer. God still hears and answers prayers.

Instil the Memory Verse

Recite all the commandments learnt so far.

Lesson - 19: NEHEMIAH BEFORE THE GREAT KING

Scripture Reading: Nehemiah 1:1-11; 2:1-9

1. Who Am I?

Choose from: Nehemiah, Artaxerxes, God

  1. I tasted the king’s wine and I was sad about Jerusalem. Who am I? __________ 👉 Nehemiah

  2. I was the king who allowed Nehemiah to go. Who am I? __________ 👉 Artaxerxes

  3. I answered Nehemiah’s prayer. Who am I? __________ 👉 God

2. Choose the Right Answer

Circle a), b) or c):

  1. What bad news did Nehemiah hear?
    a) Jerusalem was in ruins b) The king was sick c) A ship was lost 👉 a) Jerusalem was in ruins

  2. What did Nehemiah do before he spoke to the king?
    a) He ran away b) He prayed silently c) He sang 👉 b) He prayed silently

3. Right or Wrong?

Write R for right or W for wrong:

  1. Taking my sadness to God in prayer. ( ___ ) 👉 R

  2. Making a good plan after I pray. ( ___ ) 👉 R

  3. Thinking God does not hear me. ( ___ ) 👉 W

4. Discussion Questions

  1. What did Nehemiah do when he heard the bad news?

    Sample answer: He prayed to God for several months.

  2. What did the king give Nehemiah?

    Sample answer: Permission to go, building supplies and soldiers to protect him.

  3. Does God hear and answer our prayers?

    Sample answer: Yes, in his own way and time.

5. Memory Verse:

Exodus 20:13 — “You shall not murder.”

Matthew 7:7 — “Ask and it will be given to you; seek and you will find.”

Craft 1: Prayer Journal

Materials: Folded paper, stapler or string, crayons

Instructions:

  1. Fold paper into a little book.

  2. Write on the cover: “My Prayers”.

  3. Draw one thing to pray about inside.

Lesson Point: God hears when we pray.

Craft 2: Ruined City Picture

Materials: Paper, crayons, brown paper

Instructions:

  1. Draw broken walls with brown paper.

  2. Add Nehemiah praying.

  3. Write: “Lord, help us.”

Lesson Point: Nehemiah took his sadness to God.

Game 1: Pass the Prayer

How to Play:

  1. Children stand in a line.

  2. Each whispers a short prayer for a friend.

  3. The last says “Amen” aloud.

Lesson Point: We can pray for others.

Game 2: Happy Face, Sad Face

How to Play:

  1. Teacher reads a story piece.

  2. Children show a happy or sad face.

  3. Then pray together about the sad parts.

Lesson Point: God cares about our feelings.
