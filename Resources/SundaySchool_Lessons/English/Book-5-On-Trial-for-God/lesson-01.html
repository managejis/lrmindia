Naaman Visits Elisha

Download PDF Download Word

Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 5: On Trial for God

Aim

To show the children that we cannot come to God trusting in our own importance.

Scripture Reading

2 Kings 5:1-12

Memory Verse

Exodus 20:1-2

And God spoke all these words: “I am the Lord your God …”
NOTE: During this set of lessons, the children will learn the Ten Commandments (Ex 20:1-17).

Presenting the lesson

Starting point

People are proud of the country they come from. For example, if you come from Australia you will be proud to know that it is the world’s largest island. Think of the things that make you proud of your country.

Main Points of the Lesson

1. Naaman was very proud of his country, Syria. It was an important country with a beautiful capital called Damascus, and lovely rivers that made the land fertile. He also was very proud of his position as commander of the Syrian army, which had won many battles. Naaman was proud of the fact that the king of Syria thought highly of him (2 Ki 5:1a).

2. But there was one thing Naaman was not proud of. He had leprosy. In those days there was no cure for this skin disease, not even in an advanced and prosperous country like Syria (5:1b).

3. Naaman’s wife had a slave girl who came from the poor neighbouring country of Israel. She was thought to be one of the most unimportant members of Naaman’s household, but she knew where her master would find help. She told her mistress about a prophet of the God of Israel who could cure leprosy (5:2-3).

4. When Naaman’s wife told him about this he got leave of absence from the king of Syria to travel to Israel to visit this prophet. He took many valuable gifts with him (Syria was a rich country and Naaman an important man). He also took along a letter from the king himself that said who Naaman was and that ordered the recipient to cure his good servant Naaman (5:4-6).

5. However, the proud General Naaman went to the wrong person for help. He and his king thought he should go to see the king of Israel, not some prophet. When the king of Israel read the letter he became very worried, because he couldn’t cure Naaman’s leprosy. He was afraid that if he displeased the king of Syria it would give the Syrians a reason to attack Israel again (5:7).

6. God’s prophet Elisha heard what had happened and instructed the king of Israel to send Naaman to his humble house. Elisha was the one who could cure Naaman by the power of God (5:8).

7. Show Picture 1. Point out how angry Naaman looks. Elisha the prophet chose not to receive Naaman or any of his gifts. He just sent his servant out to meet the important general with some instructions that seemed like a joke: Naaman had to wash himself seven times in the River Jordan. The proud Syrian soldier despised the little country of Israel and felt offended by Elisha’s simple advice and lack of respect. Angrily, he refused to follow the instructions … and he still had leprosy (5:9-12).

Our Response to the Story

Why didn’t the prophet Elisha welcome Naaman with great respect, as one would have expected?

Why did he send a mere servant to this important man with instructions that seemed so ridiculous?

Elisha wanted to show Naaman that his important country, his wealth and his position counted for nothing before the God of Israel. God would heal Naaman only when he stopped trusting in his own abilities and power. His pride was stopping him from receiving God’s blessing.

We are sometimes too proud of what we can do ourselves, and that can stop us from receiving God’s blessings.

In the next lesson we shall see how Naaman learnt this truth.

Lesson - 1: NAAMAN VISITS ELISHA

Scripture Reading: 2 Kings 5:1-12

1. Who Am I?

Choose from: Naaman, Elisha, the servant girl

  1. I was the commander of the Syrian army, but I had leprosy. Who am I? __________ 👉 Naaman

  2. I told my mistress about a prophet in Israel. Who am I? __________ 👉 the servant girl

  3. I sent my servant to tell Naaman to wash in the Jordan. Who am I? __________ 👉 Elisha

2. Yes or No?

Write Y for Yes or N for No:

  1. Was Naaman proud of his country and his job? ( ___ ) 👉 Y

  2. Did Naaman go to the king of Israel first? ( ___ ) 👉 Y

  3. Was Naaman happy about Elisha’s advice? ( ___ ) 👉 N

3. Choose the Right Answer

Circle a), b) or c):

  1. How many times did Naaman have to wash in the Jordan?
    a) Three b) Seven c) Ten 👉 b) Seven

  2. What stopped Naaman from receiving God’s blessing?
    a) His pride b) His horse c) His hunger 👉 a) His pride

4. Discussion Questions

  1. Why do you think Naaman was angry?

    Sample answer: He was proud and expected Elisha to treat him as an important man.

  2. Why did Elisha send a servant instead of coming out himself?

    Sample answer: He wanted Naaman to learn that his wealth and position meant nothing before God.

  3. What can pride stop us from doing?

    Sample answer: It can stop us from listening to God and receiving his blessings.

5. Memory Verse:

Exodus 20:1-2 — “And God spoke all these words: “I am the Lord your God, who brought you out of Egypt, out of the land of slavery.””

Proverbs 16:18 — “Pride goes before destruction, a haughty spirit before a fall.”

Craft 1: Naaman’s Chariot

Materials: Paper, crayons, scissors, glue, a small box

Instructions:

  1. Draw a chariot and a horse on paper and cut them out.

  2. Colour them brightly like a general’s chariot.

  3. Glue the chariot to the box.

  4. Write: “God is greater than my pride.”

Lesson Point: Our importance and our riches do not impress God.

Craft 2: River Jordan Picture

Materials: Blue paper, brown paper, glue, crayons

Instructions:

  1. Tear blue paper into a wavy river.

  2. Glue it onto brown paper.

  3. Draw Naaman standing by the river.

  4. Write: “Listen and obey.”

Lesson Point: God’s way may look simple, but it is the right way.

Game 1: Proud Walk, Humble Walk

How to Play:

  1. Let the children walk across the room like proud, important people.

  2. Then let them walk like humble servants.

  3. Ask which walk would please God.

Lesson Point: God is pleased with humble hearts.

Game 2: Pass the Message

How to Play:

  1. Whisper a short message from one child to the next in a line.

  2. The last child says it aloud.

  3. Talk about how the servant girl’s message helped Naaman.

Lesson Point: Even the smallest person can help someone find God.
