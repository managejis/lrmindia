Jacob the Deceiver

Download PDF Download Word

Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 2: Mighty Men of God

Aim

To teach the children that Esau and Jacob put their own desires before God’s plan. This led to family quarrels.

Scripture Reading

Genesis 25:19-34

Memory Verse

Proverbs 3:6

[I]n all your ways acknowledge him, and he will make your paths straight.

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

Either use the following story or make up your own: Musa and Khamis were brothers. Musa had a beautiful car he had made. Khamis wanted it. Musa would not let him play with it. They began to quarrel and to fight over the car. Their mother had to stop them. They both wanted the same car.

Main Points of Today’s Teaching

1. There were two brothers who quarrelled in the Bible, too. They were more than brothers; they were twins. Their names were Esau and Jacob (Gen 25:24-26). Explain that they were Abraham’s grandsons, because they were Isaac’s sons.

2. Convey Genesis 25:21-26 in your own words. These two babies fought with each other even before they were born. Esau was the firstborn (v 25, 27). Jacob was born second (v 26-27). There was division (opposite sides) in this household; therefore, it was not a happy place (v 28).

3. Relate the story of verses 29-34. Make sure the children understand what “birthright” means.

Esau came in from the fields so hungry he thought he would die if he didn’t have something to eat right away. Jacob was cooking, and it smelled wonderful. Show picture 1. But Jacob would give Esau food only if Esau would give him the birthright in return. The birthright was the first son’s inheritance. It was God who had given Esau his special position in the family, but now all Esau could think of was food. He was hungry, and he cared more about that than about the blessing he would receive as the firstborn son. Jacob, on the other hand, wanted what was not his. Esau gave away his birthright in order to satisfy his hunger for food.

Instil the Memory Verse

Our Response to the Story

Question: “Did Esau and Jacob acknowledge God in all that they did? That is, did they remember to ask what God wanted them to do?”

Answer: “No, they did not.”

Let the class come up with some suggestions as to what happens when:

a) God’s instructions are disobeyed. b) Parent’s instructions are disobeyed.

The best way is to follow the advice in today’s memory verse.

Lesson - 1: JACOB THE DECEIVER

Scripture Reading: Genesis 25:19-34

1. Who Am I?

Choose from: Esau, Jacob, Isaac

  1. I was born first and I was a hunter. Who am I? __________ 👉 Esau

  2. I cooked the red stew and wanted my brother’s birthright. Who am I? __________ 👉 Jacob

  3. I am the father of the twins. Who am I? __________ 👉 Isaac

2. Choose the Right Answer

Circle a), b) or c):

  1. What did Esau give away for a bowl of stew?
    a) His tent b) His birthright c) His bow 👉 b) His birthright

  2. Why did Esau give it away?
    a) He was very hungry b) He was sleepy c) He was afraid 👉 a) He was very hungry

3. Right or Wrong?

Write R for right or W for wrong:

  1. Asking God what He wants me to do. ( ___ ) 👉 R

  2. Tricking someone to get what I want. ( ___ ) 👉 W

  3. Choosing food over God’s blessing. ( ___ ) 👉 W

  4. Sharing my toy with my brother. ( ___ ) 👉 R

4. Discussion Questions

  1. Why did Esau and Jacob quarrel?

    Sample answer: They both wanted what they thought was best for themselves, and did not ask what God wanted.

  2. What should Esau have done?

    Sample answer: He should have valued the blessing God gave him more than a meal.

  3. What happens when we disobey our parents or God?

    Sample answer: It brings quarrels and sadness.

5. Memory Verse:

Proverbs 3:6 — “In all your ways acknowledge him, and he will make your paths straight.”

Colossians 3:20 — “Children, obey your parents in everything, for this pleases the Lord.”

Craft 1: Bowl of Red Stew

Materials: Paper plate, red and brown paper, glue, crayons

Instructions:

  1. Colour the paper plate as a bowl.

  2. Cut small red and brown paper pieces for the stew.

  3. Glue the pieces into the bowl.

  4. Write: “Esau sold his birthright.”

Lesson Point: We should not give away God’s blessing for something small.

Craft 2: Brothers at Peace Card

Materials: Folded paper, crayons

Instructions:

  1. Draw two brothers holding hands on the front.

  2. Inside write: “Ask God first.”

  3. Give it to your brother, sister or friend.

Lesson Point: Following God’s way brings peace to families.

Game 1: Treasure Trade

How to Play:

  1. Give each child a small prize card.

  2. Offer a sweet snack in exchange for the card.

  3. Talk about who traded and why, and how Esau traded his birthright.

Lesson Point: Some things are worth more than what we want right now.

Game 2: Share or Quarrel?

How to Play:

  1. Hold up one toy and give it to two children.

  2. Let them show a quarrelling way and then a sharing way.

  3. The class votes which way is happier.

Lesson Point: Sharing brings peace; quarrelling brings sadness.
