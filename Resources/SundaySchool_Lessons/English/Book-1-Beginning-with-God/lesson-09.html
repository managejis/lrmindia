Job Lives a Godly Life

Download PDF Download Word

Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 1: Beginning with God

Aim

To teach the children that God reigns.

Scripture Reading

Job 1:1-12

Memory Verse

Psalm 34:8

Taste and see that the Lord is good; blessed is the man who takes refuge in him.

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

Ask the class what they think a person needs to be happy.

Main Points of Today’s Teaching

1. When the Bible first introduces us to Job, we read of a person who lived a very happy life. He was a good and honest man who served God (1:1).

2. Job had a family: he was married and had seven sons and three daughters (1:2).

3. Job was a very rich and respected man, who owned large herds of animals and had many servants (1:3).

4. Job’s family loved and enjoyed one another; they would often come together and hold feasts (1:4).

5. Job was a loving, caring father. He made offerings to God on behalf of his family, because he wanted God to forgive them if they had sinned (1:5). Show Picture 9.

6. But one day Satan came before the Lord’s throne. God praised Job before Satan, but Satan argued that Job only worshipped God for the good things he got out of it (1:6-11).

7. God knew that Job was a true servant who honestly only wanted to please his Lord. So God allowed Satan to take away everything Job possessed, except his health (1:12).

Instil the Memory Verse

Our Response to the Story

Many things happen in our lives, and in the world, that we do not understand. Give some current examples. Over the next three lessons we shall see how Job responded to suffering that he did not deserve and did not understand.

From today’s reading we can take courage: God is ultimately in control and limits what the evil one can do.

Lesson - 9: JOB LIVES A GODLY LIFE

Scripture Reading: Job 1:1-12

1. Count and Write

Write the number:

  1. Job had ____ sons. __________ 👉 7

  2. Job had ____ daughters. __________ 👉 3

  3. How many children did Job have in all? ____ __________ 👉 10

2. Match the Following

A B
Job A good and honest man
Satan Said Job loved God only for gifts
God Praised Job
Job’s offerings Asking God to forgive his children

Answers:
Job → A good and honest man
Satan → Said Job loved God only for gifts
God → Praised Job
Job’s offerings → Asking God to forgive his children

3. Yes or No

Write Yes or No:

  1. Was Job a rich man? ( ___ ) 👉 Yes

  2. Did Job only worship God for the good things he got? ( ___ ) 👉 No

  3. Is God stronger than Satan? ( ___ ) 👉 Yes

4. Discussion Questions

  1. What made Job a happy man?

    Sample answer: He loved and served God, and had a loving family.

  2. Why did Job make offerings for his children?

    Sample answer: He wanted God to forgive them if they had sinned.

  3. How can we show that we love God, not just His gifts?

    Sample answer: By thanking, obeying and trusting Him every day.

5. Memory Verse:

Psalm 34:8 — “Taste and see that the Lord is good; blessed is the man who takes refuge in him.”

Psalm 46:1 — “God is our refuge and strength, an ever-present help in trouble.”

Craft 1: Paper Chain Family

Materials: Paper strips, scissors, crayons, glue

Instructions:

  1. Fold a long strip of paper like an accordion and draw a person on the front.

  2. Cut around the person, keeping the folds joined at the hands.

  3. Open it to show a chain of ten children and write: “Job loved his family.”

Lesson Point: Job was a loving father who prayed for his family.

Craft 2: Thank-You Heart

Materials: Red paper, scissors, crayons

Instructions:

  1. Cut a heart shape from red paper.

  2. Draw or write three things you thank God for.

  3. Write: “God is good.”

Lesson Point: We love God for who He is, not only for what He gives.

Game 1: Count Job’s Animals

How to Play:

  1. Put pictures or toy animals around the room.

  2. Children go in turns to collect and count them.

  3. Talk about how God gave Job many animals, but Job loved God more.

Lesson Point: Job’s riches did not matter more to him than God.

Game 2: Thank-You Ball

How to Play:

  1. Sit in a circle and pass a soft ball.

  2. The child who catches it says one thing they thank God for.

  3. Continue until everyone has had a turn.

Lesson Point: A thankful heart trusts that God is good.
