Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 1: Beginning with God

Aim

To teach the children that God made us as well as the world we live in.

Scripture Reading

Genesis 1:1-27

Memory Verse

Genesis 1:1

In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

Ask the class to name all the various things they can see around them: chairs, benches, trees, sky, people, etc. Somebody must have made all these things. Who made them?

Main Points of Today’s Teaching

1. God, the great Spirit who is everywhere and who knows all things, created everything around us. A carpenter may have made the benches and chairs, but God made the trees for the wood and gave mankind the skill to do such work.

2. Ask the class to put their hands over their eyes, then ask them what they can see. Nothing. God, who is the all-powerful Spirit, created everything out of nothing. No-one else can do that. A carpenter can make a bench, but he cannot create it out of nothing; he has to have some wood to start with.

3. Show Picture 1. God created all the things you see in this picture. Point out the various objects and let the children name them as you go.

4. Before God began his work of creation, all was dark. Then God said, “Let the light come.” When God had spoken, there was light everywhere. God was very pleased with what he had created on that first day. There was light during the day and darkness during the night.

5. On the second day, God gave the earth its shape, which is like an orange. (If oranges are unavailable, replace the example with another suitably shaped fruit.) Show the class an orange. God then separated the earth from the sky.

In the same way, describe what was created on the other days. Repeat every time, as the Bible does, that God was pleased with his creation: It was good.

6. On the third day the dry land, seas, plants and trees were created.

7. On the fourth day the sun, moon and stars were created.

8. On the fifth day the birds and sea creatures were created.

9. On the sixth day the land animals and the first people were created. God breathed the spirit of life into mankind. The Bible calls them Adam and Eve.

Instil the Memory Verse

Put or hold up Picture 1 where the class can see it as they learn the verse.

Our Response to the Story

God created the world for us to look after. How can we do this? Ask the class for examples, and name some yourself. Here are some suggestions:

  • Whenever you cut down one tree, plant two young trees.

  • Don’t throw away water you have finished with; use it to water plants.

  • Make compost out of vegetable peelings and ashes and leaves.

  • Put dangerous rubbish like broken glass and plastic in a special place so no-one will be hurt.

Lesson -1 : HOW EVERYTHING BEGAN

Scripture Reading: Genesis 1:1-27

1. Match the Days of Creation

Day What God Created
1 A. Sun, Moon, Stars
2 B. Sky and Water
3 C. Land, Plants, Trees
4 D. Light and Darkness
5 E. Birds and Sea Creatures
6 F. Animals and Humans
7 G. God rested

2. Fill in the Blanks

"In the beginning, God created the _______________________ and the __________________________________."
(Answer: heavens, earth)

"God saw all that He had made, and it was very ______________."
(Answer: good)

3. Discussion Questions

  • What is your favorite thing that God made?

  • Why do you think God rested on the 7th day?

  • How can we take care of God’s creation?

4. Memory Verse :

  • Genesis 1:1 – “In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth.”

  • Psalm 19:1 – “The heavens declare the glory of God; the skies proclaim the work of His hands.”

A Simple Craft Idea for Students:

To help students easily remember this verse, have them paint one half of a simple paper plate black (representing darkness) and the other half yellow (representing light), and then write the verse in the middle.

An Activity for Students:

Call six students to the front of the class, give each one a day (1 to 6), and ask them to draw what was created on that day on a piece of paper. Finally, combine all the drawings to create a large "Creation Poster."

Creation Wheel (Craft)

Ask the students to divide a circular piece of paper into six sections. Have them write the numbers 1 through 6 in each section and draw what was created on each corresponding day.

For example:

1. A candle (light)

2. Clouds (sky)

3. A flower or a tree (land/plants)

4. The sun and the moon

5. A fish and a bird

6. A human face

Classroom Game: "God Said" Game

This is similar to the "Simon Says" game.

• How to play: If the teacher says, "God said 'Let there be light'," the students should spread their hands out as if showing light.

• If the teacher says, "God said 'Let there be birds'," they should flap their arms like wings.

• If the teacher says only the word without saying "God said" (for example: "Fish swim"), the students should not perform any action. Those who make a mistake are out!

• Objective: This game will help students understand that God created the world through His word, and that we must obey His word.
