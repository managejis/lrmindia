Power Through Prayer

Download PDF Download Word

Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 8: Acts of the Holy Spirit

Aim

To teach the children the power of prayer.

Scripture Reading

Acts 12:12-18

Memory Verse

1 John 3:22

[We] receive from him anything we ask, because we obey his commands and do what pleases him.

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

Tell the class how a prayer of yours was answered. Perhaps the answer was not exactly what you had prayed for. God often answers our prayers in a different way from what we expected, but it still is an answer. Whether God replied “Yes” or “No” or “Later”, God wills what is best for us.

Main Points of the Lesson

1. Show Picture 11. Ask questions that refer to it. For example: Who is the man between the soldiers? What is the angel telling him to do?

With these kinds of questions you can review the memory verse of Lesson 11, Psalm 32:7.

2. When the angel left Peter on the city street and he realised that God had freed him from prison, he went to a house where he knew the Christians met regularly. They were praying when he knocked on the door (12:12-13).

3. Rhoda, who came to answer the door, was so happy to hear Peter’s voice that she forgot to let him in. She ran off to tell the others that he was safe and free (12:14)!

4. Most of the Christians gathered there did not believe Rhoda’s good news, even though Peter’s release from prison was exactly what they had been praying for (12:15). Peter had to keep on knocking for some time before they came to open the door (12:16).

Show Picture 12. Point out the surprise on their faces. Do you see the man with the stick in his hand? He probably thought it could not be Peter at the door, but someone who wanted to play a trick on them.

5. Peter told the congregation what had happened and asked them to tell the other apostles that he was safe. Then he left the house. If he had stayed, he might have put everybody there in danger, as the authorities would come looking for him (12:17).

Our Response to the Story

Our prayers are answered if we pray according to God’s will. Therefore, the power of prayer depends on our obeying and following Jesus. Sometimes the answer is greater than we dared to expect, as was the case with those Christians who prayed for Peter.

What would you like to pray for? Possible answer: the healing of a sick relative.

Include their requests in your closing prayer and encourage the children to go on praying for these things at home in the coming week.

Lesson - 12: POWER THROUGH PRAYER

Scripture Reading: Acts 12:12-18

1. Who Am I?

Choose from: Rhoda, Peter, the Christians

  1. I was so happy that I forgot to open the door. I am ____________. __________ 👉 Rhoda

  2. We were praying but could not believe it was Peter. We are ____________. __________ 👉 the Christians

  3. I kept on knocking at the door. I am ____________. __________ 👉 Peter

2. Odd One Out

Circle the one that does not belong:

  1. Pray, thank, sing, grumble 👉 grumble

  2. Yes, no, later, never listen 👉 never listen

  3. Knock, door, house, boat 👉 boat

3. Prayer: Right or Wrong? R/W

Write R for right, W for wrong:

  1. God sometimes answers “Yes”, “No” or “Later”. ( ___ ) 👉 R

  2. We pray only when we are in trouble. ( ___ ) 👉 W

  3. God knows what is best for us. ( ___ ) 👉 R

4. Discussion Questions

  1. Why did the Christians not believe Rhoda?

    Sample answer: It was almost too good to be true.

  2. What can we learn about prayer?

    Sample answer: God hears and answers, sometimes in surprising ways.

  3. What would you like to pray for?

    Sample answer: Answers will vary, such as a sick friend or family.

5. Memory Verse:

1 John 3:22 — “And receive from him anything we ask, because we keep his commands and do what pleases him.”

Matthew 7:7 — “Ask and it will be given to you.”

Craft 1: Praying Hands

Materials: Paper, crayons, scissors

Instructions:

  1. Trace both hands next to each other.

  2. Cut them out and glue them together.

  3. Write one thing to pray for.

Lesson Point: God hears our prayers.

Craft 2: Knock at the Door

Materials: Card, crayons, scissors

Instructions:

  1. Make a folding door from card.

  2. Draw Peter on the inside.

  3. Write: “God answers prayer.”

Lesson Point: God answered the Christians’ prayers.

Game 1: Yes, No, Later

How to Play:

  1. The teacher reads simple requests.

  2. Children say what a loving Father might answer.

  3. Talk about why God’s answers are best.

Lesson Point: God always answers our prayers.

Game 2: Knock, Knock

How to Play:

  1. A child knocks and says “It’s Peter!”

  2. Another child acts surprised and opens the door.

  3. Everyone says: “Thank You, God!”

Lesson Point: God surprises us with answers.
