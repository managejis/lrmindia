A Message for Kings

Download PDF Download Word

Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 8: Acts of the Holy Spirit

Aim

To teach the children that Paul did not hesitate to witness about the Lord Jesus to powerful rulers.

Scripture Reading

Acts 25:13-27; 26:1-32

Memory Verse

Romans 1:16

I am not ashamed of the gospel, because it is the power of God for the salvation of everyone who believes …

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

How would you feel if you were told you were going to meet a very important person like the governor or the president? How would you prepare yourself?

Main Points of the Lesson

1. After Paul had spent two years in prison without being put on trial, a new Roman governor took over from the one who had put Paul in prison. The new governor, Festus, wanted to know why the Jews wanted Paul killed. He found out it was because Paul taught that Jesus, whom they had killed, was alive again (Acts 25:19). However, under Roman law, a person could not be put to death for his or her religious beliefs.

2. Festus did not know what to do with Paul. Then Agrippa, whom the Romans had made king of the Jews, and his wife Bernice came to visit Festus. Festus asked Agrippa’s advice about Paul. Agrippa replied that he first wanted to hear what Paul had to say for himself (25:14-22).

3. It was a grand occasion when Paul was brought before King Agrippa. Show Picture 22. Describe who was present (25:23). Point out the different people and Paul in his chains.

4. The governor, Festus, introduced Paul to the king (25:24-27).

5. Paul was not afraid of anyone. He was polite to the king, but he spoke freely about Jesus Christ. Read the memory verse out loud. No-one would frighten Paul into remaining silent about the good news that Jesus had come to save all who would believe in him.

6. Paul gave his testimony to the king. Agrippa was a Jew, so he knew about the expected Messiah, the Saviour God had promised he would send into the world (26:2-18).

7. Paul spoke about his work and witnessed to people everywhere (26:19-23).

8. Paul’s testimony offered everybody the opportunity to accept Jesus Christ, but Festus thought Paul was mad. Agrippa did show some interest, but let it go in the end. The result of the hearing was that Paul would go to Rome, the capital of the Roman Empire, to be tried by the emperor himself. That meant that he would take the Gospel to the most important city in the world (26:24-32)!

Our Response to the Story

The Gospel is meant for all people, whoever they are.

Let us now spend some time praying for everyone in our country who holds a powerful position. Let us pray that there will be opportunities for Christians to witness to those leaders who have not accepted Jesus Christ. Pray, too, for those who are suffering because of their Christian beliefs.

Lesson - 22: A MESSAGE FOR KINGS

Scripture Reading: Acts 25:13-27; 26:1-32

1. Who Am I?

Choose from: Festus, Agrippa, Paul

  1. I was the new Roman governor. I am ____________. __________ 👉 Festus

  2. I was a king who heard Paul’s story. I am ____________. __________ 👉 Agrippa

  3. I was a prisoner but not ashamed of the gospel. I am ____________. __________ 👉 Paul

2. Choose the Right Answer

Circle a), b) or c):

  1. Who did Paul speak to?
    a) Only friends b) A king c) Nobody 👉 b) A king

  2. Where was Paul sent next?
    a) Rome b) Egypt c) Greece 👉 a) Rome

  3. What was Paul not ashamed of?
    a) The gospel b) His chains c) His job 👉 a) The gospel

3. True or False

Write T for True or F for False:

  1. Paul was polite and spoke clearly. ( ___ ) 👉 T

  2. Paul kept quiet to please the king. ( ___ ) 👉 F

  3. The good news is for everyone, even leaders. ( ___ ) 👉 T

4. Discussion Questions

  1. Why was Paul brave before the king?

    Sample answer: He believed God’s message was most important.

  2. Why do leaders need to hear about Jesus?

    Sample answer: Because God loves them too.

  3. Who can we pray for?

    Sample answer: Our leaders and those who suffer.

5. Memory Verse:

Romans 1:16 — “For I am not ashamed of the gospel, because it is the power of God that brings salvation to everyone who believes.”

Psalm 119:46 — “I will speak of your statutes before kings and will not be put to shame.”

Game 1: King’s Court

How to Play:

  1. One child is the king, another is Paul.

  2. Paul says one sentence about Jesus.

  3. Swap roles.

Lesson Point: Paul spoke about Jesus to a king.

Game 2: Not Ashamed

How to Play:

  1. Teacher says a Bible fact.

  2. Children say proudly “I am not ashamed!”

  3. End with the memory verse.

Lesson Point: We are not ashamed of the good news.
