Gideon Destroys the Idols in Israel

Download PDF Download Word

Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 3: Victory through God

Aim

To show the children that God is far greater than any idol made of wood or stone. We worship the living God, while idols are powerless and dead.

Scripture Reading

Judges 6:25-32; Isaiah 44:9-20

Memory Verse

Isaiah 44:9

All who make idols are nothing, and the things they treasure are worthless.

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

Ask the class to name some things that have power (examples: a motorcar or an electric generator which can be used to give light). Then ask them to name some other things which have no power (examples: a rock or a piece of wood).

Main Points of the Lesson

1. Our God is the source of all power, but the Israelites forgot that. They also forgot all the wonders God had performed to save them and to triumph over their enemies. They started worshipping idols carved from wood or stone. These were the gods of the Canaanite tribes, their neighbours, who were the people of Israel’s enemies.

2. God told Gideon to destroy these idols and their places of worship. The stones he had to use to build an altar to God, and the wood to make a fire for bringing God a burnt offering (6:25-26).

3. Gideon was so afraid that he took 10 of his servants and did the work at night, when no-one would see them. Show Picture 14. Gideon wanted to obey God, but he was scared of what other people would say (6:27).

4. When the townspeople found out what had happened to their gods, they were very angry, just as Gideon had feared. They wanted to kill him (6:28-30).

5. But Gideon’s father protected him. “If Baal and Asherah” (the names of these idols) “really have any power, they will punish Gideon themselves,” he said. Of course, nothing happened, because Baal and Asherah were just figures of wood and stone. They were not real, living gods (6:31-32).

Our Response to the Story

Nearly 20 years ago a few Christians in the Kerio Valley in Kenya prepared a runway for an aeroplane. They had to cut down trees and move big boulders. They wanted a Christian medical safari to come to that isolated area and bring the people the gospel of Christ as well as medical help. In the process the Christians destroyed two big anthills which were believed to be sacred and powerful.

The Christians knew there was no real power in these anthills, so they were not afraid to break them down. Today there are many Christian churches in that area.

Lesson - 14: GIDEON DESTROYS THE IDOLS IN ISRAEL

Scripture Reading: Judges 6:25-32; Isaiah 44:9-20

1. Odd One Out

Circle the one that has no power:

  1. A car a generator a wooden idol 👉 a wooden idol

  2. God Baal Jesus (circle the idol) 👉 Baal

  3. A rock a king an idol of stone (circle the one that is alive) 👉 a king

2. Choose the Right Answer

Circle a), b) or c):

  1. When did Gideon tear down the idols?
    a) At night b) At noon c) At breakfast 👉 a) At night

  2. Who protected Gideon?
    a) His father b) The king c) Barak 👉 a) His father

3. Alive or Dead? A/D

Write A for alive or D for dead (no power):

  1. The living God. ( ___ ) 👉 A

  2. An idol made of wood. ( ___ ) 👉 D

  3. An idol made of stone. ( ___ ) 👉 D

4. Discussion Questions

  1. Why did Gideon feel afraid?

    Sample answer: He thought the people would be angry with him.

  2. What happened to Baal when Gideon broke him?

    Sample answer: Nothing. He was only wood and stone.

  3. What can we put first instead of idols?

    Sample answer: God alone.

5. Memory Verse:

Isaiah 44:9 — “All who make idols are nothing, and the things they treasure are worthless.”

Exodus 20:3 — “You shall have no other gods before me.”

Craft 1: God Alone Sign

Materials: Card, crayons

Instructions:

  1. Write: “God alone.”

  2. Draw a bright crown above.

  3. Hang it on your wall.

Lesson Point: God is greater than any idol.

Craft 2: Broken Idol Picture

Materials: Paper, crayons, glue

Instructions:

  1. Draw a wooden statue in pieces.

  2. Glue the pieces apart.

  3. Write: “Idols have no power.”

Lesson Point: Idols cannot help us.

Game 1: Power or No Power?

How to Play:

  1. Hold up pictures of things.

  2. Children shout “power!” or “no power!”

  3. End with “God has all power!”

Lesson Point: Only God has real power.

Game 2: Brave at Night

How to Play:

  1. Everyone whispers a brave word.

  2. Take turns saying: “I will obey God even if I am scared.”

  3. Clap softly together.

Lesson Point: God helps us obey when we are afraid.
