Two People Come Before God

Download PDF Download Word

Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 7: Jesus Lord and Saviour

Aim

To show the children the way God wants us to come to him in prayer.

Scripture Reading

Luke 18:9-14

Memory Verse

No new memory verse

Review the passage learnt so far.

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

Choose three of the class members and prepare a little play to start the lesson. One child must be a shopkeeper and the other two customers. The customers must take turns to come to the shop and buy something. The first customer must have a proud and rude attitude, while the second one must be courteous. Let the class see clearly who receives better service from the shopkeeper.

Main Points of the Lesson

1. Jesus told a story about two people who also acted very differently from each other. Both went to the temple to pray. One was a Pharisee, which means he was a religious leader and respected by the people. The other was a tax collector. That was a job that often made a person an outcast in Jesus’ days. It was bad enough that the tax collectors worked for the hated Roman government, but they often were dishonest and made people pay more than they had to (Lk 18:10).

2. The Pharisee stood where everyone could see him. His prayer was all about how good he was and how glad he was that he was better than others, who were greedy or dishonest or immoral. He certainly was not like that wicked tax collector over there. He boasted about all the good things he did for God. Even though God’s law only asked a person to fast (go without food) once a year, he fasted twice a week (18:11-12). The Pharisee sounded as though he didn’t need God at all, as if God should thank him for being so wonderful instead of the other way round!

3. The tax collector’s prayer and his attitude was very different. He knew that he was a sinner coming before God, who is holy. He asked God humbly to help and save him (18:13). Show Picture 19. Point out once again the differences between the two men.

4. Jesus told his listeners that God heard and answered the tax collector’s prayer. He went home with his sins forgiven and his relationship with God restored. The Pharisee just went home with his pride (18:14).

Our Response to the Story

How do you behave when the headmaster comes into your classroom at school? Give the children time to respond. We are not proud and boastful in front of him. If we behaved like that, he would think that we never needed his help or leadership.

We could say God is like our chief, although he really is so much more. He is our Creator and loving Father, too. But we are sinners who disobey him, therefore we need to ask for God’s forgiveness first of all, when we talk to him. Only when everything is put right between us, God will listen to the other things we have to say.

Proud people trust in themselves and their own abilities instead of trusting in God, like Naaman did before he was healed. Remind the children of Naaman’s story if necessary. God will not help people who think they need no help (18:9, 14).

Lesson - 19: TWO PEOPLE COME BEFORE GOD

Scripture Reading: Luke 18:9-14

1. Who Am I?

Choose from: the Pharisee, the tax collector, God

  1. I boasted about how good I am. Who am I? __________ 👉 the Pharisee

  2. I said, “God, have mercy on me, a sinner.” Who am I? __________ 👉 the tax collector

  3. I hear the prayer of the humble. Who am I? __________ 👉 God

2. Proud or Humble? P/H

Write P for proud or H for humble:

  1. I say sorry to God. ( ___ ) 👉 H

  2. I think I am better than others. ( ___ ) 👉 P

  3. I ask God to help me. ( ___ ) 👉 H

3. Circle the Right Words

Circle the right word:

  1. The Pharisee stood (proudly / quietly). 👉 proudly

  2. The tax collector looked (down / up) and was sorry. 👉 down

  3. He went home (forgiven / angry). 👉 forgiven

4. Discussion Questions

  1. What was wrong with the Pharisee’s prayer?

    Sample answer: He boasted and did not ask for help.

  2. Why did God hear the tax collector?

    Sample answer: He was humble and honest.

  3. How should we come to God in prayer?

    Sample answer: Humbly, saying sorry and trusting him.

5. Memory Verse:

John 1:5 — “The light shines in the darkness, but the darkness has not understood it.”

James 4:6 — “God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble.”

Craft 1: Humble Prayer Hands

Materials: Paper, crayons

Instructions:

  1. Trace and cut out praying hands.

  2. Write: “God, help me.”

  3. Colour the hands.

Lesson Point: God hears humble prayers.

Craft 2: Two Men Picture

Materials: Paper, crayons

Instructions:

  1. Draw two men praying.

  2. Make one stand tall and one bow low.

  3. Write: “God hears the humble.”

Lesson Point: God loves the humble.

Game 1: Proud and Polite

How to Play:

  1. Two children act proud and polite at a pretend shop.

  2. The class decides who is treated better.

  3. Talk about humility.

Lesson Point: Humility pleases God.

Game 2: Say Sorry First

How to Play:

  1. Teacher reads short stories of wrong-doing.

  2. Children say, “God, forgive me.”

  3. End with a quiet prayer.

Lesson Point: We should ask forgiveness first.
