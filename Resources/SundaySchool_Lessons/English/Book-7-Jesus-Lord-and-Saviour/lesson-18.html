The Friend at the Door

Download PDF Download Word

Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 7: Jesus Lord and Saviour

Aim

To teach the children that God wants us to bring all our requests and worries to him.

Scripture Reading

Luke 11:1-13

Memory Verse

John 1:13

[C]hildren [of God are] born not of natural descent, nor of human decision or a husband’s will, but born of God.
NOTE: Repeat the memory verse for Lesson 17.

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

What time do you go to bed at night? Most of us go to bed when darkness has fallen. By midnight we certainly are in bed, unless there’s a celebration or some other special reason for staying up late.

Main Points of the Lesson

1. Jesus told a story about someone who received a visitor at midnight. The visitor had been travelling, so he probably hadn’t eaten all day. He was hungry, but the house owner had no bread left to give his guest. What a terrible thing! A guest must not go to bed hungry! Therefore the man went to his friend’s house to borrow three loaves of bread, even though it was very late (Lk 11:5-6).

2. Describe what happened (11:7-8).

Note: It may be easier for the children to follow if you give the two main characters names. It is a lively story and also very short, and so would be a good one to act. You could act the parts of the two main characters yourself if you wish. Keep this activity short enough so that you still have time for “Our Response to the Story” at the end of the lesson.

Show Picture 18. We see that the friend eventually had to get up and lend the man the bread in order to stop him knocking on the door. The man was not ashamed to keep on asking for what he needed, and his persistence was rewarded.

Our Response to the Story

Jesus often went off on his own to pray to God the Father. One day when he returned from his prayers his disciples asked him to teach them how to pray. In answer to their question Jesus told today’s story (11:1-2a, 5a). An ordinary friend will help us when we ask, even if it is only because we keep on asking. Therefore, we can be absolutely certain of God’s help, because he loves us far better than any other person could ever love us. God will give us the help we need, even if he seems to delay sometimes.

If a small child likes a particular food very much they will go on and on eating it. Someone has to stop them or they will make themselves sick. It is not always good for us to have what we ask for, when we ask for it. God knows what is good for us and when the time is right. Like a human parent, God gives his children good gifts only. Most of all God wants us to have his Spirit in our lives (11:11-13).

A spider takes a long time to spin its web. Sometimes the web breaks loose from where the spider attached it. Then the spider has to start again. Sometimes this happens several times, and each time the spider tries again. The spider never gives up. We should never give up on prayer.

Come let us think of some things we need to pray about. Give the class time to respond, then say a short prayer. I want you to keep praying for this during the week that lies ahead.

Instil the Memory Verse

Lesson - 18: THE FRIEND AT THE DOOR

Scripture Reading: Luke 11:1-13

1. Complete the Sentences

Word box: bread, knock, midnight

  1. The man visited his friend at ____________. __________ 👉 midnight

  2. He asked for three loaves of ____________. __________ 👉 bread

  3. He kept on ____________ at the door. __________ 👉 knocking

2. Choose the Right Answer

Circle a), b) or c):

  1. Why did the man need bread?
    a) A guest had arrived b) He was lazy c) He wanted to play 👉 a) A guest had arrived

  2. What does the story teach about prayer?
    a) Never ask b) Keep on asking c) Ask once 👉 b) Keep on asking

3. Unscramble the Words

Use the clue to help you:

  1. YARPER (talking to God) → __________ 👉 PRAYER

  2. KOCNK (what the man did at the door) → __________ 👉 KNOCK

  3. DNEIFR (the man went to his ...) → __________ 👉 FRIEND

4. Discussion Questions

  1. What did the man do when his friend said no?

    Sample answer: He kept on asking.

  2. Why can we be sure God will help us?

    Sample answer: He loves us even more than a friend can.

  3. What would you like to pray for this week?

    Sample answer: Family, friends or something that worries me.

5. Memory Verse:

John 1:13 — “... children born not of natural descent, nor of human decision or a husband’s will, but born of God.”

Matthew 7:7 — “Ask and it will be given to you; seek and you will find; knock and the door will be opened to you.”

Craft 1: Door Hanger

Materials: Card, crayons, scissors, string

Instructions:

  1. Cut a door shape from card.

  2. Write: “Keep on praying.”

  3. Add a string loop to hang it.

Lesson Point: We should never give up on prayer.

Craft 2: Prayer Loaves

Materials: Brown paper, crayons

Instructions:

  1. Cut three loaves from brown paper.

  2. Write one thing to pray for on each.

  3. Keep them and pray all week.

Lesson Point: God hears our requests.

Game 1: Knock, Knock

How to Play:

  1. One child knocks on a pretend door.

  2. The class asks, “What do you need?”

  3. The child names a need; everyone prays briefly.

Lesson Point: We can always come to God with our needs.

Game 2: Never Give Up

How to Play:

  1. Draw a spider web on paper.

  2. Children trace over a line again and again.

  3. Say: “Keep on praying.”

Lesson Point: The spider never gives up, and we should not give up praying.
