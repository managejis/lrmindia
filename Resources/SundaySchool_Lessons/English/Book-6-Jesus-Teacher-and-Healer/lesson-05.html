Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 6: Jesus Teacher and Healer

Aim

To show the children how Jesus began to teach his disciples to pray.

Scripture Reading

Matthew 6:5-15; Luke 11:1; Nehemiah 1:1-11; 2:1-9

Memory Verse

Matthew 6:6

“[W]hen you pray, go into your room, close the door and pray to your Father, who is unseen. Then your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you.”

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

Give some examples of what people do to attract someone else’s attention. They can shout, wave their arms, bang a drum or blow a horn. Let the children give suggestions, too. Everyone will look at a person who does these kind of things, to see what is going on.

Main Points of the Lesson

1. Show Picture 5. Ask the class questions about it. Who are the people on the right looking at? Give the children time to respond. Yes, they are watching this man, who is richly dressed and praying aloud at the street corner. Jesus saw religious leaders praying like this so that everyone would notice them and think they were very godly. The praise of other people was the only reward for such people’s prayers, Jesus said (Matt 6:5).

2. Jesus said we are to pray like this other man in Picture 5. He spends time alone with God in his room. He is not trying to impress other people with his prayers (Matt 6:6). Note: (a) The picture is a little misleading. In the Bible the second man prays behind a closed door (6:6). In the picture, the door is open; and the man is kneeling behind it, so he is not showing off to his neighbours.

(b) It is the custom of Jewish men to pray with their heads covered.

3. Tell the story of Nehemiah as an example of the way Jesus was teaching people to pray. Nehemiah’s visitors from Jerusalem brought him very sad news. The capital city of his homeland was still in ruins. Nehemiah prayed where he could be alone, telling God how sad that made him and asking God what he should do (Neh 1:1-10). He did this for four months!

4. Only after he had spoken and listened to God Nehemiah went to the king of Babylon, whose servant he was. When the king asked him why he looked so sad, Nehemiah told him about Jerusalem. The king gave him leave to visit the city and also gave him building materials and protection for the journey (2:1-9). That was far more than what Nehemiah had prayed for.

Instil the Memory Verse

Our Response to the Story

This lesson will have a greater impact if you, the teacher, can share a story from your own life about an answer to prayer.

Lesson - 5: PRAYING TO GOD

Scripture Reading: Matthew 6:5-15; Luke 11:1; Nehemiah 1:1-11; 2:1-9

1. Match the Following

A B
Religious leaders Prayed loudly to be seen
Jesus’ way Pray alone to your Father
Nehemiah Prayed for Jerusalem
The king Gave him help and wood

Answers:
Religious leaders → Prayed loudly to be seen
Jesus’ way → Pray alone to your Father
Nehemiah → Prayed for Jerusalem
The king → Gave him help and wood

2. Count and Write

Write the answer:

  1. Nehemiah prayed for ____ months. __________ 👉 four

  2. Jesus said to close the ____________ when you pray. __________ 👉 door

  3. God is our ____________ in heaven. __________ 👉 Father

3. Right or Wrong Prayer? R/W

Write R for right or W for wrong:

  1. Praying quietly and telling God everything. ( ___ ) 👉 R

  2. Praying loudly so people say, “How good!” ( ___ ) 👉 W

  3. Listening to God after I pray. ( ___ ) 👉 R

4. Discussion Questions

  1. Where did Jesus say we can pray?

    Sample answer: In a quiet place, alone with God.

  2. What made Nehemiah sad?

    Sample answer: Jerusalem was still in ruins.

  3. What can you thank God for in prayer?

    Sample answer: Answers will vary, such as family, food and friends.

5. Memory Verse:

Matthew 6:6 — “When you pray, go into your room, close the door and pray to your Father, who is unseen. Then your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you.”

1 Thessalonians 5:17 — “Pray continually.”

Craft 1: Praying Hands

Materials: Paper, scissors, crayons, glue

Instructions:

  1. Trace your hands side by side, with fingers together.

  2. Cut them out and fold them at the wrist.

  3. Write inside: “Thank You, Father.”

Lesson Point: We can talk to God anywhere, at any time.

Craft 2: Prayer Door Hanger

Materials: Card, scissors, crayons, string

Instructions:

  1. Cut a door-shaped card with a hole at the top.

  2. Colour it and write: “Quiet time with God.”

  3. Hang it on a door at home.

Lesson Point: Jesus taught us to pray in secret.

Game 1: Quiet Corner

How to Play:

  1. Walk to a quiet corner of the room one at a time.

  2. Each child says a very short silent prayer.

  3. Return to the circle without speaking.

Lesson Point: God hears us when we pray quietly.

Game 2: Prayer Pass

How to Play:

  1. Sit in a circle and pass a soft ball.

  2. The child who holds it says “Thank You, God, for ...”.

  3. Pass it on until everyone has prayed.

Lesson Point: Prayer is simply talking to God.
