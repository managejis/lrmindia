Humility Instead of Revenge

Download PDF Download Word

Teacher’s Guide · Look, Listen & Live – Book 6: Jesus Teacher and Healer

Aim

To show the children that followers of Jesus Christ should not take revenge but leave judgment to God, as no-one of us is blameless.

Scripture Reading

Matthew 5:38-42; Luke 6:27-36; 1 Samuel 24:1-22.

Memory Verse

Matthew 6:15

“[I]f you do not forgive men their sins, your Father will not forgive your sins.”

Presenting the Lesson

Starting point

Have you ever seen somebody giving someone else a push from behind? What usually happens? The first person will make the other one lose his/her balance and maybe fall down. If someone hurts us in some way, most of us want to pay them back – often in a worse way. It would be striking if two children acted this in front of the class. Take care that nobody is hurt!

Main Points of the Lesson

1. Jesus taught us very differently.

Show Picture 4. Jesus used the example of a soldier who abused a man. He beat the man and stole his coat. What should that man do? Jesus said he should not hit the soldier back. Instead of beating the soldier, the man had to offer him his shirt, too! Jesus teaches that his followers must always try to make a situation better. Taking revenge, by starting a fight, for example, will only make things worse. This is Christ’s way, and it is very different from the way in which the world behaves!

Talk about the examples in Matthew 5:38-41.

Note: People who harm others should be punished according to the country’s laws. But many times the wrongdoer is not caught or the wrong person is held responsible. God is the only reliable judge. We should leave punishment of other people to God and take care not to sin ourselves.

Instil the Memory Verse

2. Tell the story of David finding Saul in the cave (1 Sam 24:1-22). David could have taken revenge but he did not. He knew it was in God’s hands. David knew that he himself lived by the grace of God, not by his own righteousness.

Our Response to the Story

It is very difficult for us to obey this teaching of Jesus! But revenge is a sin; it means we think we can judge, as if we were God himself.

Here is a true story of a man who did obey Jesus: He was put in prison because he was a Christian and he was treated very badly. His cell was too small for him to even sit down and he was given only salty rice to eat. The authorities burnt his Bible. When he was finally released, a policeman watched his home all the time. To the policeman’s surprise, the former prisoner treated him well and gave him a Bible to read. The policeman was converted.

Lesson - 4: HUMILITY INSTEAD OF REVENGE

Scripture Reading: Matthew 5:38-42; Luke 6:27-36; 1 Samuel 24:1-22

1. Pay Back or Forgive? P/F

Write P for pay back or F for forgive:

  1. Someone pushes me, so I push harder. ( ___ ) 👉 P

  2. Someone is unkind, so I speak kindly to him. ( ___ ) 👉 F

  3. I leave it to God to be the judge. ( ___ ) 👉 F

  4. I tell everyone bad things about the one who hurt me. ( ___ ) 👉 P

2. Choose the Right Answer

Circle a), b) or c):

  1. Whom did David find sleeping in a cave?
    a) King Saul b) Goliath c) Elijah 👉 a) King Saul

  2. What did David do to Saul?
    a) He hurt him b) He did not hurt him c) He ran away 👉 b) He did not hurt him

  3. Who is the best judge?
    a) Me b) My friend c) God 👉 c) God

3. Yes or No

Write Yes or No:

  1. Did Jesus say we should make things worse by fighting? ( ___ ) 👉 No

  2. Did Jesus teach us to be kind to people who hurt us? ( ___ ) 👉 Yes

  3. Does taking revenge make a problem bigger? ( ___ ) 👉 Yes

4. Discussion Questions

  1. What do many people want to do when someone hurts them?

    Sample answer: They want to hurt that person back.

  2. What does Jesus teach us to do instead?

    Sample answer: To be kind and forgive, and leave judging to God.

  3. Why did David not hurt Saul?

    Sample answer: He trusted God to be the judge.

5. Memory Verse:

Matthew 6:15 — “If you do not forgive men their sins, your Father will not forgive your sins.”

Romans 12:21 — “Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good.”

Craft 1: Kind Hands

Materials: Paper, pencil, scissors, crayons

Instructions:

  1. Trace both your hands on paper and cut them out.

  2. On each hand write one kind thing you can do.

  3. Stick the hands on a card.

Lesson Point: We can answer hurt with kindness.

Craft 2: Peace Dove

Materials: White paper, scissors, crayons, string

Instructions:

  1. Fold white paper and cut a dove shape with wings.

  2. Colour the eye and write “Peace” on a wing.

  3. Tie a string and hang it up.

Lesson Point: Followers of Jesus choose peace, not revenge.

Game 1: Cave Story

How to Play:

  1. Two children act the story: David quietly finds Saul and walks away.

  2. The class says “David did not take revenge!”

  3. Change actors and tell it again.

Lesson Point: David left the judging to God.

Game 2: Kind Reply

How to Play:

  1. The teacher says an unkind sentence, such as “You are slow!”.

  2. A child answers with a kind reply.

  3. Everyone claps for the kindest answer.

Lesson Point: A kind answer stops a quarrel.
